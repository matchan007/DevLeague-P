01

The bottom line

Four arm's-length land sales inside the district carry this study. Together they moved 646,389 square feet of Waikiki land for $850 million.

Area-weighted land value $1,315/sf Total dollars ÷ total land area across the four primary comps. The single most defensible number here.
Median comp $1,600/sf Midpoint of the four. Runs high because two of the four are small parcels.
Observed range $1,137–2,000 Low: the 10.3-acre Royal Hawaiian block. High: a 33,500 sf corner on Kalakaua.
Land appreciation 9.5%/yr Bank of Hawaii Waikiki Center ground: $119/sf in 1995 → $2,000/sf in 2026. Same dirt, 31 years.

Three findings matter more than the headline average, and they should shape how you use this study.

Finding 01 — Parcel size drives price more than date does

Across the four primary comps there is no usable time trend. A 2019 sale ($1,791/sf) sits above a 2025 sale ($1,137/sf). What separates them is size and frontage, not the calendar. Small parcels on Kalakaua clear near $2,000/sf; ten-acre blocks clear near $1,150/sf. If your parcel is under ~35,000 sf, the small-lot comps govern — not the headline average.

Finding 02 — The Hawaiian trusts are the sellers

Kamehameha Schools and Queen Liliʻuokalani Trust are on the sell side of three of the four primary comps ($783M of the $850M). Buyers are overwhelmingly Japanese and Korean institutional capital plus U.S. ground-lease REITs. This is a structural rotation of Waikiki's fee ownership, not a series of one-off trades — which means more leased-fee product is likely to come to market.

Finding 03 — Nearly every comp is a leased-fee sale

All four primary comps conveyed land already encumbered by a long-term ground lease. That is not the same asset as a clean, deliverable, developable site. A leased-fee buyer purchases an income stream and a reversion; a developer buys the right to build now. Depending on the lease's rent and remaining term, unencumbered fee land can be worth meaningfully more than these figures, and a below-market ground lease makes it worth less. There is no verified vacant, unencumbered, ready-to-develop land sale in the district in the ten-year window.

What this is, and what it isn't

Read this before you put any of these numbers in front of a lender or a partner.

Data provenance — read this

This study was compiled from published transaction reporting: trade press, seller press releases, SEC filings, and news coverage of recorded deeds. It was not compiled from primary records. Direct access to the City & County Real Property Assessment Division, the Hawaii Bureau of Conveyances, and the HoLIS parcel/zoning GIS layers was blocked by network policy in the environment this was built in.

Consequence: land areas, TMKs, and closing dates carry the confidence of a news report, not a title search. Section 09 lists exactly what to pull to convert this from a well-sourced desktop study into something an appraiser or a lender will underwrite. No figure below is invented — but several are single-sourced, and they are marked.

Boundary. The Waikiki Special District as adopted in the Land Use Ordinance: the Ala Wai Canal on the mauka and Ewa sides, Kapahulu Avenue on the Diamond Head side, and the ocean makai. One transaction in the table (the Gold Coast boutique hotel at 2885 Kalakaua) sits outside that line and is flagged and excluded from every conclusion.

What counts as a comp. You asked for land value extracted from all sales including improved property, which is correct for this market — Waikiki has effectively no vacant land. The comps are graded into three classes:

ClassWhat it isHow it's used
Land Fee or leased-fee interest in the land itself conveyed separately from the building. Primary. These four drive every conclusion. $/sf is a true land rate.
Improved Land and buildings conveyed together in one price. Ceiling test. $/sf of land includes the building, so it sets an upper bound on land, never a land rate.
Leasehold Only the tenant's interest under a ground lease conveyed. No land changes hands. Excluded from valuation. Shown for market context and ownership history only. Dividing these prices by land area produces a meaningless number.
Fee simple vs leased fee — companion note

Every primary comp below conveyed a leased fee: the landowner’s half of a ground lease, not clean unencumbered land. A separate note explains the difference, shows the Hawaii convention linking ground rent to land value, and provides a converter that turns any leased-fee price here into its fee simple equivalent — The Ground Lease Split. The Interest column in the table records what changed hands in each row.

Extraction. Where a sale is improved, no depreciated-cost allocation has been run — that requires building cost and condition data not available here. The improved figures are therefore presented as gross $/sf of land with the building included, which is why they read lower than the pure-land comps rather than higher: the improved assets in this sample are older, functionally dated buildings on interior blocks.

05

What actually moves the number

Size and frontage, in that order. Not the year.

Land rate falls as parcel size rises

Land / leased-fee Improved (ceiling)

Parcel size on a log scale. The dashed line is the fitted size–price relationship across the four land comps; with n=4 it is an indication of direction, not a regression you should underwrite to.

The size effect. Big Waikiki blocks sell at a discount per foot because the pool of buyers who can write a $500 million check is tiny, and because a ten-acre assemblage already has its highest and best use built on it. Small corner parcels sell at a premium because they are scarce, because they complete assemblages, and because a neighbouring owner will pay more than the open market to control the lot next door.

The frontage effect. Holding size roughly constant, Kalakaua Avenue frontage and beach proximity carry the premium. The clearest read in the data: ʻAlohilani's oceanfront 2.5 acres at 2490 Kalakaua cleared $1,791/sf, while Hilton Waikiki Beach's 1.27 acres two blocks mauka at 2500 Kuhio — a smaller parcel, sold in the same year, by the same seller — cleared $1,410/sf. That roughly 27% gap is location, isolated about as cleanly as this market ever offers.

The gap in the evidence

Every primary comp sits on Kalakaua or Kuhio. There is no verified land comp in the interior Apartment Precinct — the Ala Wai side, the residential blocks off Ena Road and Olohana Street. If your parcel is in there, this study does not price it, and anyone extrapolating the $1,315/sf figure onto an Ala Wai lot is guessing. Interior apartment-zoned land in Waikiki should be expected to trade well below the resort corridor, but the discount is not measured here. Section 09 tells you how to close that gap.

06

Zoning

In Waikiki the precinct governs what you can build, and what you can build governs what the land is worth. Two parcels of identical size across the street from each other are not the same asset.

Every parcel in the district carries two layers: an underlying Land Use Ordinance zoning district, and a Waikiki Special District precinct laid over it. The precinct is usually the binding constraint, and it is the one that decides whether transient vacation units — hotel use, the highest-value use in Waikiki — are permitted as of right.

PrecinctHotel / transient useWhat it means for land value
Resort Mixed Use Permitted as of right. TVUs and B&Bs both allowed. The premium precinct. All four primary comps sit in the resort corridor. Access to the PD-R (Planned Development – Resort) permit path, which is where the density upside lives.
Resort Permitted as of right. Full hotel entitlement. Values track the Resort Mixed Use precinct.
Apartment Not as of right. Residential use; PD-A permit path only. Materially lower land value. No hotel income to capitalise. This is the precinct with no comps in this study — treat any number applied here as unsupported.
Apartment Mixed Use Not as of right. Residential with limited commercial. Between Apartment and Resort in value; still unmeasured in this dataset.
Public n/a Parks, rights-of-way, civic. Not a market category.
Height limit — Diamond Head end 220ft Waikiki runs a graduated height ceiling that steps down toward Diamond Head.
Height limit — Ala Moana end 350ft The Ewa end carries the tallest envelope in the district.
PD-R base density 4.0FAR Maximum project floor area ratio under the Planned Development – Resort path.
PD-R ceiling 5.0FAR Where existing FAR already exceeds 3.33, density may rise up to 20%, capped at 5.0. Above 5.0, existing FAR is the cap.
Why this matters to your pricing

A land rate is really a price for buildable floor area. At 4.0 FAR, land at $1,315/sf is $329 per buildable square foot; at 5.0 FAR the same land is $263. If your parcel already carries an FAR above 5.0, that grandfathered density is the single most valuable thing about it — existing FAR becomes your cap, and it cannot be recreated. Confirm the existing FAR before you price the land.

07

Who is selling, who is buying

And what each of these parties traded before — the five-year history behind the counterparties you'd be negotiating against.

The sell side is a small, identifiable group of Hawaiian trusts monetising legacy ground positions. Kamehameha Schools closed the $510M Royal Hawaiian ground in November 2025, six weeks after selling roughly 500 acres under the Four Seasons Hualālai on Hawaiʻi Island for about $400M. Queen Liliʻuokalani Trust sold two Waikiki grounds in 2019 alone — ʻAlohilani at $195M in September and Hilton Waikiki Beach at $78M in August — under a repositioning strategy its trustees set in 2015. It has publicly stated that its remaining Waikiki position, more than four acres under the Waikiki Beach Marriott, is not for sale.

The buy side is cross-Pacific institutional capital and ground-lease specialists. Daisho (Tokyo) took the Royal Hawaiian ground. Shoei (Japan) bought the ground under its own building at the Bank of Hawaii Waikiki Center. Safehold, the ground-lease REIT founded and managed by iStar, took ʻAlohilani. On the leasehold side the pattern repeats: Mirae Asset (Seoul) bought the Hyatt Regency leasehold from Blackstone for $755.6M in 2016; Commerz Real (Germany) took the majority of the Pacific Beach leasehold around 2017; Financial Products Group (Japan) bought the Queen Kapiolani in December 2022.

The negotiating read

If you are acquiring, you are most likely buying from either a trust with a fiduciary mandate and no time pressure, or a family partnership holding a legacy ground position. Neither is a distressed seller. The Bank of Hawaii ground is the instructive case: Kapuahelani LP held that dirt from 1995 to 2026 and sold it to its own lessee — the party with the most to gain from controlling it and the least ability to walk away. That is how the $2,000/sf top of the range got set, and it is why that comp should be treated as an upper bound rather than a market midpoint.

What this number does not include

This is land value only. An acquisition cost is not the same thing. Not included: any building on the site; transaction costs (title, escrow, Hawaii conveyance tax, brokerage, legal); entitlement and permitting cost and the carry to get through it; demolition and site work; tenant buyouts or relocation; and — if the site is encumbered by a ground lease — the cost of the leasehold interest, which as the ʻAlohilani history above shows can exceed the land itself by two to three times.

09

Verify before you rely on this

Nine lookups convert this desktop study into something an appraiser, a lender, or a partner will accept. All are public records; none cost more than a fee and an afternoon.

  1. Pull the TMK and land area for every comp above. Confirm each parcel's actual square footage rather than the acreage a reporter rounded. This alone can move a $/sf figure by 10–15%. City & County of Honolulu, Real Property Assessment Division — parcel search by address or TMK
  2. Pull the recorded deed for each comp and read the conveyance tax stamp. Hawaii's conveyance tax is computed on actual consideration, so the stamp confirms price — including for the Queen Kapiolani sale, where the price was never released. State of Hawaii Bureau of Conveyances / Land Court — document search by grantor, grantee, or TMK
  3. Read the ground lease on every leased-fee comp. Rent, remaining term, reset dates, and reversion terms determine what the buyer actually paid for. Without these, the four primary comps are not strictly comparable to each other. Recorded lease or memorandum of lease, Bureau of Conveyances
  4. Confirm your own parcel's precinct and underlying zoning. Resort Mixed Use versus Apartment is the largest single value fork in this study. Honolulu DPP zoning map / HoLIS parcel viewer; LUO Ch. 21-9, Waikiki Special District
  5. Confirm your parcel's existing FAR and height envelope. Grandfathered density above 5.0 FAR cannot be recreated and is worth pricing separately. Honolulu DPP; Waikiki Special District Design Guidelines
  6. Close the Apartment Precinct evidence gap. Ask a Honolulu commercial brokerage to run interior Waikiki land and apartment-building sales from the MLS and CoStar. That data is licensed and was not available for this study. A local CRE brokerage with CoStar and Honolulu Board of Realtors access
  7. Verify the two flagged rows. The Waikiki Trade Center price and date, and the Kuhio Avenue studio-apartment assemblage sale, are each single-sourced with no confirmed land area. RPAD parcel record plus recorded deed
  8. Check for encumbrances on your target. Easements, shoreline setback, the Waikiki Special District design review, historic designation, and any existing tenancies all reduce what you can deliver. Preliminary title report; DPP records
  9. Commission a state-certified appraisal before you commit capital. This study is research, not an appraisal, and it is not a substitute for one. Hawaii state-certified general appraiser with Waikiki hospitality experience
